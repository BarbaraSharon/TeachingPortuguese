## Prepare for Lisbon with practical Portuguese

Lisbon travel is easier when you can recognise everyday Portuguese in transport, cafés, shops and accommodation. This guide helps Brazilian Portuguese learners understand phrases they may hear in Portugal and plan respectful, confident interactions.

## Brazilian and European Portuguese in Lisbon

Barbara Sharon teaches Brazilian Portuguese and can explain differences you may notice while travelling. European Portuguese often has reduced unstressed vowels, a different rhythm and some different everyday vocabulary. These comparisons help you understand local speech without presenting this page as European Portuguese tuition.

| Situation | Brazilian Portuguese | European Portuguese |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Train | Trem | Comboio |
| Bus | Ônibus | Autocarro |
| I am speaking | Estou falando | Estou a falar |
| Formal you | Você | O senhor / A senhora |
| Please | Por favor | Se faz favor |

## Useful phrases for Lisbon

- **Olá, bom dia** — Hello, good morning.
- **A minha conta, por favor** — My bill, please.
- **Onde fica a estação?** — Where is the station?
- **Quais são os horários?** — What are the opening hours?
- **Um pastel de nata, por favor** — A custard tart, please.
- **Perdi o meu passaporte** — I lost my passport.
- **Pode repetir, por favor?** — Could you repeat that, please?

## A simple preparation checklist

Before travelling, practise introducing yourself, asking for directions, checking prices, ordering food and explaining a dietary need. Save the local names for transport and key places in your itinerary, and listen for the pronunciation differences described above.
