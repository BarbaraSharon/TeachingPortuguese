## Decide what is missing

Conversation practice and structured teaching solve different problems. If you regularly run out of basic language, do not understand common patterns or repeat errors without knowing why, you probably need structured instruction. If you know the language on paper but hesitate, translate slowly or struggle with unpredictable replies, guided conversation may be the missing practice.

## Compare the emphasis

| Structured course | Conversation class |
| --- | --- |
| Introduces and sequences new language | Extends use of language you already know |
| Balances speaking with explanation and focused exercises | Gives more time to spontaneous listening and speaking |
| Tracks progression across lessons | Builds flexibility across topics and speakers |
| Corrects within a planned learning path | Corrects patterns that emerge in conversation |

A conversation class should still have purpose and facilitation. Simply placing several learners in a call does not ensure equal speaking time, useful topics or constructive feedback.

## A combined approach

Use a course to learn a pattern, practise it in controlled examples and receive correction. Then use a conversation session to retrieve that pattern while telling a story, solving a problem or responding to follow-up questions. Bring recurring difficulties back to the structured lesson.

Complete beginners can join supported conversation activities, but they may need models, preparation and shorter turns. More experienced learners can handle greater unpredictability.
