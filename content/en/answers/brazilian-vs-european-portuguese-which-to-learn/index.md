## Let your real destination decide

Choose the variety used by the people and places most important to you. A learner speaking with a Brazilian partner has a different priority from someone moving to Lisbon. If your interest comes from music, film or literature, notice which variety dominates what you enjoy.

When there is no clear external goal, choose the variety for which you have a suitable teacher, good listening material and regular opportunities to practise. Consistency makes the early stages easier.

## What stays shared and what changes

Brazilian and European Portuguese share the writing system and much of their grammar and vocabulary. They differ in pronunciation, rhythm, common vocabulary, forms of address and some everyday structures. Natural-speed listening can therefore feel very different even when written sentences look familiar.

You do not need to treat them as unrelated languages. Build a strong primary variety, then learn recurring differences so you can adjust your expectations and understand more speakers.

## Avoid mixing without a reason

Using Brazilian audio, European spelling guidance and an unrelated app sequence can make a beginner unsure which form to practise. Ask teachers and courses to state their main variety. Mixing becomes easier later, once you can recognise why a form differs.
