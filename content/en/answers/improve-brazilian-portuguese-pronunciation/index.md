## Start with rhythm and a short phrase

Pronunciation is more than producing separate consonants and vowels. Brazilian Portuguese has rhythm, stress and connected speech that influence how words sound inside a sentence. Begin with a phrase short enough to hear and repeat accurately.

Use this sequence:

1. Listen without reading and notice the stressed part.
2. Listen again while following the text.
3. Repeat slowly without changing the rhythm into English.
4. Record one attempt.
5. Compare one feature rather than judging your whole accent.
6. Repeat the phrase with a different name, place or time.

## Work on sounds inside real language

Nasal vowels, open and closed vowel qualities, some forms of **r**, and sounds written with combinations such as **lh** or **nh** may be unfamiliar to an English speaker. Practise the physical movement, but return quickly to a useful word and sentence. The goal is to recognise and produce the sound while communicating.

Spelling can help, but avoid reading Portuguese as if it followed English sound rules. Use reliable audio whenever you add new vocabulary.

## Record with a clear purpose

A recording is most useful when you know what to compare. Choose stress, one vowel, a final sound or the connection between two words. Make two attempts, apply one change and listen again. Save occasional recordings of the same speaking task to notice progress over time.

## Train listening and pronunciation together

If you cannot hear a contrast, it is difficult to reproduce it consistently. Alternate between identifying a sound and saying it. Listen to several speakers so you learn that natural Brazilian Portuguese includes regional variation rather than one acceptable voice.

## Aim for clear speech, not a copied identity

An accent is not a failure. Prioritise features that affect understanding and confidence. Rhythm, word stress and key vowel differences may have more impact than perfecting every minor detail. A teacher can help decide what matters for your speech.
