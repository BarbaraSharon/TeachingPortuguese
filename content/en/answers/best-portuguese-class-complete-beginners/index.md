## Look for a beginner pathway

A beginner class should not assume that you already understand pronunciation, verb terminology or how Portuguese sentences are built. It should introduce a small amount of language, let you hear and use it, and return to it in new situations.

Useful early outcomes include greeting someone, introducing yourself, asking a simple question, understanding a likely reply and repairing the conversation when you miss something.

## Compare formats honestly

- **Private lessons:** Useful when your pace, language background or goal needs individual attention.
- **Group classes:** Useful when you want several speaking partners and can learn at a shared pace.
- **Live online classes:** Useful when location or travel makes attendance difficult, provided there is real interaction and reliable audio.
- **Self-paced material:** Useful for review and repetition, but it cannot listen and respond like a live teacher.

The right format is the one that gives you sufficient guidance and that you can sustain. A demanding programme you abandon is less useful than a realistic routine with regular contact.

## Questions to ask

Ask whether complete beginners speak from the first lessons, how pronunciation is taught, how corrections work, what practice is expected and whether the language variety is Brazilian or European Portuguese. Also confirm group level, current schedule and what happens if you need to review a topic.
