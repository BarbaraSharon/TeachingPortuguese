## Similarity helps most with recognition

Portuguese and Spanish developed from Latin and share many related words. A Spanish speaker may understand the topic of a written Portuguese text before taking lessons. Familiar verb concepts, gender and agreement can also reduce the amount of completely new grammar.

Recognition can create false confidence, however. Knowing what a word probably means on a page does not guarantee that you will recognise it at natural speed or pronounce it so a Portuguese speaker understands.

## Pronunciation creates a major difference

Brazilian Portuguese has vowel qualities, nasal sounds, rhythm and sound reductions that differ from Spanish. A learner who reads every Portuguese word with Spanish pronunciation may be understood in some contexts but will also develop habits that make listening and natural speech harder.

Use Portuguese audio from the beginning. Repeat complete phrases, notice stressed syllables and compare how familiar-looking words actually sound. The [pronunciation guide](/en/answers/improve-brazilian-portuguese-pronunciation/) provides a focused routine.

## Watch for false friends and different usage

Some words look similar but differ in meaning, tone or frequency. Other words share a basic meaning but are used in different expressions. Avoid translating one word at a time. Learn vocabulary with the sentence and situation in which it appears.

Grammar also contains familiar-looking patterns that do not behave identically. Treat similarities as a useful hypothesis to check, not as proof that a Spanish construction can be transferred directly.

## A learning plan for Spanish speakers

1. Use your shared vocabulary to begin reading accessible Portuguese.
2. Give listening and pronunciation extra attention from the first week.
3. Keep a list of recurring Spanish transfers, not every isolated mistake.
4. Practise the corrected Portuguese form in several personal sentences.
5. Speak with feedback rather than waiting for reading comprehension to become speaking automatically.
