## Recognition is not the same as retrieval

When listening or reading, the surrounding sentence helps you recognise a word. When speaking, you must find that word without seeing it, choose a structure and produce the sounds while another person waits. It is normal for comprehension to develop ahead of spontaneous speech.

Passive activities can widen the difference. Watching programmes, using subtitles and rereading notes may improve recognition, but they do not automatically train rapid retrieval.

## Turn familiar input into active speech

Use material you already understand and change what you do with it:

1. Listen to a short exchange without reading.
2. Summarise the main idea aloud in simple Portuguese.
3. Repeat one useful sentence and change a detail.
4. Answer the same question in two different ways.
5. Return to the task later without looking at the original.

This reduces the mental load because the topic and vocabulary are familiar while the act of producing language is new.

## Practise reusable sentence patterns

Instead of memorising a complete speech, practise flexible frames such as expressing a preference, explaining a reason, asking for clarification or describing something that happened. Replace one part each time. Reusable patterns help you start a sentence while deciding what comes next.

## Make conversation easier before making it faster

Choose a limited topic and prepare key words, but do not script every reply. Ask your speaking partner or teacher to allow a little thinking time. Practise clarification phrases so a missing word does not end the conversation. Speed usually becomes less of a problem when retrieval is more familiar.

## Use feedback without stopping every sentence

Constant interruption can make a hesitant speaker more cautious. A teacher can note recurring patterns, decide which errors affect understanding and review them after a short speaking task. The learner can then repeat part of the conversation using the correction.
