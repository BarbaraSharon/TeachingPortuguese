## Translation is a stage, not a failure

Beginners naturally use a language they already know to understand a new one. The difficulty appears when every response requires composing a complete English sentence and converting each word. Portuguese word order, verb choices and expressions do not always map directly, so the process becomes slow and unreliable.

## Build direct connections

- Link **objects and actions** to Portuguese words without naming them in English first.
- Learn **complete chunks** such as ways to give an opinion, ask for clarification or buy thinking time.
- Answer the **same question in several ways** instead of memorising one script.
- Describe a simple picture or daily action using only language already available to you.
- Retell a short story from keywords rather than translating a written English paragraph.

Start with brief tasks. Speed is not the first goal; reliable retrieval is. Once a response is available, repeat it with one changed detail and then use it in a less predictable exchange.

## Use English deliberately

English can still help compare grammar or confirm an important meaning. Use it as a tool, then return to Portuguese examples. Avoid writing a full English script for every conversation, because that practises translation more than spontaneous speaking.

## Barbara’s teaching perspective

Barbara’s approach connects grammar and vocabulary with real conversation and personal goals. Guided questions, role-play and feedback give learners repeated opportunities to retrieve corrected Portuguese rather than only recognise it.

If you understand more than you can say, follow the [active-retrieval exercises](/en/answers/understand-portuguese-but-cannot-speak/). Combine them with the [between-lesson routine](/en/answers/practice-between-portuguese-lessons/) and use a speaking class or private lesson to practise responding without a prepared translation.
