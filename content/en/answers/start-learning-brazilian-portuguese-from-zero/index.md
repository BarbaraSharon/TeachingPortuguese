## Build a small usable foundation

Begin with language that forms a conversation: greeting someone, giving your name, saying where you are from, asking a simple question and recognising a likely reply. Learn a repair phrase such as asking someone to repeat more slowly, because real conversations rarely follow a perfect script.

## A four-stage starting sequence

1. **Hear and copy:** Notice Brazilian Portuguese rhythm, stressed syllables and a few important sounds inside complete phrases.
2. **Build patterns:** Reuse frames such as “I am…”, “I like…” and “I need…” with personal vocabulary.
3. **Understand replies:** Practise short questions with two or three realistic answers instead of rehearsing only your own line.
4. **Have a mini-conversation:** Combine greetings, information and follow-up questions, then repeat it with one detail changed.

You can move through these stages repeatedly; they are not fixed weeks or promised milestones.

## Keep grammar connected to meaning

Learn enough grammar to understand the sentence patterns you are using. Notice gender, agreement and common verb forms, but do not wait to master every conjugation before speaking. Corrections become easier to remember when they solve a problem you just experienced.

## Barbara’s beginner approach

Barbara starts from the learner’s purpose and language background, then combines speaking, listening, pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar and Brazilian cultural context. This allows a complete beginner to build communication while understanding how the language works.
