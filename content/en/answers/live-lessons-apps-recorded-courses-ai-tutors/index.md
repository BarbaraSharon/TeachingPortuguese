## Match each tool to a job

| Format | Useful for | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live lesson | Real conversation, diagnosis, explanation and feedback | Requires a shared time and active participation |
| Recorded course | Ordered explanations and repeatable demonstrations | Cannot respond to your individual speech |
| Language app | Short repetition and habit-building | May fragment language into isolated exercises |
| AI tutor | Generating prompts, role-play ideas and low-pressure practice | Output can be wrong, unnatural or inconsistent |

The choice is not necessarily exclusive. A learner might review vocabulary in an app, follow a recorded explanation, use an AI tool to generate extra questions and then test the language in a live conversation.

## Keep human judgement in the loop

Do not assume that confident-looking AI output is correct Portuguese or appropriate for the situation. Avoid sharing sensitive personal information, and verify important language with a reliable dictionary, published source or qualified speaker. An AI tool also cannot observe your full learning history unless you provide it, and it may fail to notice why an error keeps returning.

Live teaching is most valuable when the teacher listens, adapts and gives you another chance to apply feedback. Simply moving a lecture to a video call does not create that benefit.

## Barbara’s teaching perspective

Barbara’s online lessons are live rather than a self-paced recorded product. Her published approach combines speaking, listening, pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar and cultural context around the learner’s purpose. Independent tools can support review between those sessions without replacing the human conversation.
