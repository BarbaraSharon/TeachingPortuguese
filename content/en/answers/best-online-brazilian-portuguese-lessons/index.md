## Decide whether you need live teaching

Online learning can describe a recorded course, an app, group sessions or one-to-one video lessons. If your priority is speaking and correction, look for live interaction. Recorded material can support review, but it cannot hear your pronunciation, respond to your questions or adapt an explanation in the moment.

## Use an honest comparison framework

No provider is automatically best for every learner. Compare these six factors:

1. **Goal fit:** Does the course address conversation, travel, work, family communication or your particular level?
2. **Teacher background:** Are qualifications and the language variety clearly stated?
3. **Speaking time:** Will you actively use Portuguese rather than only watch explanations?
4. **Feedback:** Does the teacher identify patterns and help you apply corrections?
5. **Structure:** Is there a clear relationship between lessons and independent practice?
6. **Practical fit:** Do the format and available times work across your time zone and routine?

Student testimonials and current external reviews can support a decision, but they do not replace a discussion of your own goal.

## Private and group lessons serve different needs

Private lessons can concentrate on one learner’s pronunciation, pace and purpose. Group lessons add interaction with other learners and shared activities. A conversation group may suit learners who already have enough foundation to practise speaking regularly. Review the [Portuguese Speaking Club](/en/portuguese-speaking-club/) separately from a structured level-based course.
